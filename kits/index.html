POLE BARN KITS AND WHERE THEY STOP

What is included

What a pole barn kit includes at $10 to $30 per square foot, what it never includes, and when buying materials as a package is actually cheaper.

Price this barn

A pole barn kit is a materials package: everything the building is made of, delivered to your site, with nothing done to your site.

That definition sounds obvious written down, and it is the source of nearly every complaint about kits — because the price looks like a building price and behaves like a shopping list.

What is in a pole barn kit

Structure: posts or laminated columns, trusses, purlins and girts. Envelope: steel roofing and wall panels, trim, ridge cap, closures. Fixings: screws, brackets, fasteners sized for the panels supplied. Sometimes: engineered drawings stamped for your snow and wind load. Check this one specifically — it decides whether your permit office will look at your paperwork.

Wood pole barn kits, metal pole barn kit, building kits — same box

Sellers label the identical package four or five ways, and the words describe the material, not a different product. Wood pole barn kits have timber trusses and columns. A metal pole barn kit means steel cladding, which nearly all of them have anyway. Metal truss pole barn kits are the ones with steel trusses instead of timber — that is a real difference, and the only one of the four labels that changes what arrives. Pole barn building kits is just the long form of the same phrase.

Read past the label to the specification: span, sidewall height, truss spacing, steel gauge, drawings. Two listings with different names and the same five numbers are the same building.

Enclosed, lean-to and loft kits

The other way kits are labelled is by what is added to the bare shell. An enclosed pole barn kit closes all four walls, as opposed to an open-sided shed for hay or equipment; it costs more because there is more steel and more girt. Pole barn lean to kits add a single-slope roof off one sidewall — cheap covered space, because the existing wall carries one side of it. Pole barn kits with loft add a floor system over part of the span, and that one is not a small upgrade: a loft is live load the trusses were not originally sized for, so it changes the engineering, not just the parts list.

What is never in the box

Four things, and together they routinely cost more than the kit.

The concrete. $4 to $6 per square foot standard, $8 to $12 frost-proof. The doors. An overhead door big enough for a tractor is its own line item. Site preparation. $7,500 to $15,000 before delivery: levelling, drainage, access. The permit. Including the stamped drawings, if the kit does not supply them.

Pole barn trusses: the part that sets the price

The truss is the only engineered component in the building, and it is where the money and the constraints sit. It spans the full width with nothing underneath, so the width in the name — the first number — is what a truss supplier is quoting on.

Three things move a truss price: the span, the snow load it is designed for, and the spacing. Pole barn truss spacing is the bay — 8 to 12 feet apart, instead of every two feet like stick framing. That is why the frame has so few parts, and why each part is larger than people expect.

Metal trusses for pole barn frames

The pole barn truss options are timber, steel, and timber with steel plates at the joints. Timber is the default and the cheapest at ordinary spans. Metal trusses for pole barn frames arrive as a welded assembly and span further for the same depth, which is why they show up on wide buildings — and why the same building in steel is quoted by a different set of suppliers.

Trusses are also sold on their own, without the rest of the kit. Listings for 30 ft pole barn trusses for sale and 40 ft pole barn trusses for sale are common, and they are worth knowing about for a repair or an extension: you are buying the one engineered component and sourcing the ordinary lumber locally. For a whole building it rarely wins — the kit price includes freight on everything at once.

What this means in practice: widening a building costs more per foot than lengthening it. Adding 10 feet of length adds another bay of the same components. Adding 10 feet of width re-engineers every truss in the building.

What the third number means: 40x60x16

Kit sellers quote sizes with three numbers. A 40x60x16 pole barn kit is sixty feet long, forty across and sixteen feet to the eave — and that third number is the sidewall height, measured to the eave rather than to the ridge.

It matters more than either of the first two, because it is the one dimension that cannot be changed later. Width can be extended with a lean-to, length by adding bays. Sidewall height is set when the posts go in the ground, and raising it afterwards means pulling the building apart.

Twelve to fourteen feet is the working range for anything with a tractor and a raised loader; ten is the practical floor for vehicles. If a quote does not state the third number, it is not a quote for the same building as the one next to it.

Pole barn kits with prices, by size

$10 to $30 per square foot. The spread inside one size is steel gauge, truss spacing and whether drawings are included — not brand prestige.

Pole barn package prices are quoted two ways, and the two are not comparable. Per square foot is the honest one, because it survives a change of size. Per building is the one in the advertisement, and it is usually the smallest specification the seller offers at that footprint. Cheap pole barn packages are almost always the second kind: the number is real, but it is a base height, thinner steel and no drawings, and the difference is added back at order time.

Pole barn kits for sale: three kinds of seller

Three kinds of seller, and the price means something different in each.

Farm and building suppliers sell packages designed around their own truss and steel supply. The price is usually complete for the shell and stated per building, and it is the easiest kind to compare.

Big-box retailers advertise pole barn kits as a catalogue line. The headline figure is often the materials for a bare shell at a base height, with doors, upgraded steel and any engineering priced separately.

Regional post-frame builders sell kits as a sideline to their build business. Their packages tend to be the most complete and the least comparable, because the specification is theirs.

Whatever the source, compare the same four things: the sidewall height, the truss spacing, what steel gauge is included, and whether stamped drawings come with it. Two kits for the same footprint differing by thousands almost always differ on that list.

When a kit is worth it

The entire saving is labour: $6 to $12 per square foot that you are doing yourself. On a 40x60 that is $14,400 to $28,800, which is real money.

It is only a saving if you can actually do it. Setting posts plumb and lifting trusses needs machinery and at least two people. If you end up hiring assembly at $5 to $10 per square foot, most of the advantage goes back.

DIY pole barn kits: what you are taking on

DIY pole barn kits are not a different product — they are the same package, sold to someone who is not a builder. What changes is everything around the box. You need a way to move bundles off a flatbed, a way to set columns plumb and hold them there while the concrete goes in, and a way to get trusses forty feet up and level. A telehandler for a week is the usual answer, and it belongs in the budget.

Two more things people find out late: the delivery is kerbside unless you paid for otherwise, and the clock on any material warranty starts at delivery, not at the day you finish.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

What are metal pole barn trusses?

Steel trusses used instead of the usual timber ones. They span further and arrive as a welded assembly, and they turn up in wide buildings where a timber truss would need to be very deep. The rest of the building is unchanged.

Wood or steel truss pole barn — which?

Timber is the default and the cheaper option at ordinary spans. Steel starts to make sense past the point where a timber truss gets deep and heavy, which is also the point where post-frame stops being obviously cheaper than a steel building.

What is in pole barn packages?

The same list as any kit: posts, trusses, purlins, girts, roof and wall steel, fasteners and trim, sometimes stamped drawings. «Package» usually signals that a seller has bundled doors or a specification level into it — check which.

Are small pole barn kits available?

Yes, down to about 200 square feet. Below that the fixed costs — prep, delivery, permit — dominate the price, and a prefab shed is usually the cheaper answer.

What is included in a pole barn kit?

Posts or laminated columns, trusses, purlins and girts, steel roofing and siding, and the fasteners to put them together. Better kits add engineered drawings. What is not included, on nearly every kit sold: the concrete slab, the overhead doors, site preparation, and the permit.
