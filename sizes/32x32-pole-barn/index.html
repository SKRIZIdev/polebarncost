32x32 pole barn cost in 2026: shell, kit and finished

Updated 17 Aug 2026 · 4 min read ·By the Pole Barn Cost editorial team
Short answer

A 32x32 pole barn is 1,024 square feet — 32 feet across the gable end and 32 feet deep, which is 3 bays at about 11 feet. As a bare shell it runs $12,800 to $25,600; finished, the range widens to $20,500 to $61,400. A concrete floor adds $4,100 to $6,100 on top, and contractor labour accounts for $6,100 to $12,300 of the total.

Rates are Fixr's 2026 contractor cost data applied to 1,024 square feet. Figures are rounded to the nearest hundred — an estimate calculated from floor area is not accurate to the dollar, and presenting it that way would read as a quote.

A 32x32 pole barn gives you 1,024 square feet under one roof. That is enough for two vehicles with room to walk between them, or a tractor with implements.

Same floor area can mean two different buildings, so the shape matters as much as the total: 32 feet across by 32 feet deep is square, which means an open floor with no obvious grain to it, and the shape that costs most per square foot to span.

Written either way: a 32x32 pole barn and a 32 x 32 pole barn are the same building, and quotes use both spellings.

How a 32x32 lays out

Dimension This building What it decides
Clear span 32 ft Truss size, and whether anything stands in the middle
Depth 32 ft a single depth of vehicles
Bays 3 at ~11 ft Where posts land, and where a door can go
End wall 32 ft Fits a single 12-foot overhead door

This span is the common range for post-frame trusses, so nothing about the frame is special order.

Door width is the constraint people meet last and regret first: an opening has to sit inside a bay, so the bay spacing above sets what is possible without extra framing. The figures for vehicles are arithmetic, not a survey — about 20 feet of depth per vehicle and three feet to walk between rows.

The gap between shell and finished is not a quality difference. It is insulation, lining, electrics and doors — and on this footprint it is $48,600 of difference, which is why a quote without a specification tells you nothing.

The two numbers people miss

Concrete. $4,100 to $6,100 for a standard slab. The building does not need it structurally, so it is genuinely optional — and that is exactly why it drops out of a mental estimate and reappears as a surprise.

Site prep. Levelling, drainage and access run $7,500 to $15,000 before anything is delivered, and that figure does not scale down much with a smaller building.

Is a kit worth it at this size?

A kit at $10,200 to $30,700 against a finished building at $20,500 to $61,400 looks like an enormous saving, and the difference is almost entirely labour — $6,100 to $12,300 of it.

Whether that is a saving depends on whether you have two people, a way to lift trusses and several free weekends. What it definitely does not include: the slab, the overhead door, and the stamped drawings your permit office will ask for.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

How much does a 32x32 pole barn cost?

$20,500 to $61,400 for the finished building, at $20 to $60 per square foot across 1,024 square feet. A bare shell sits at the bottom of that range; the top assumes insulation and interior finishing.

What does a 32x32 pole barn kit cost?

$10,200 to $30,700, at $10 to $30 per square foot. A kit is materials — posts, trusses, cladding and fasteners. It is not the concrete, the doors, the permit drawings or the labour.

How much is a concrete floor for a 32x32 pole barn?

$4,100 to $6,100 for a standard slab at $4 to $6 per square foot, and roughly double that for a frost-proof slab. A pole barn does not need a slab structurally, which is why it so often falls out of the estimate.

What fits in a 32x32 pole barn?

1,024 square feet holds two vehicles with room to walk between them, or a tractor with implements, but the shape decides the layout: 32 feet across the end wall takes a single 12-foot overhead door, and 32 feet of depth gives a single depth of vehicles. Most buildings this size end up used as a two-bay garage, a workshop, or equipment storage.

How many bays does a 32x32 pole barn have?

3, at about 11 feet each — posts go in every 8 to 12 feet, and the building length divides into that. Bay spacing is what limits where a door can go: a 12-foot opening needs a bay wide enough to take it.
