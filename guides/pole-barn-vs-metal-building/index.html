Pole barn vs metal building, shed, garage and stick built

Updated 18 Aug 2026 · 5 min read ·By the Pole Barn Cost editorial team
Short answer

A pole barn carries its roof on posts set into the ground, so it needs no continuous foundation and no load-bearing walls. A metal building carries the same roof on a steel frame, usually bolted to a slab. Stick built carries it on studs standing on a poured foundation. That single difference — what holds the roof up — decides the foundation, the span, the finish and most of the price gap between them.

Four buildings can end up looking identical from the road and cost very different money. What separates them is not the cladding. It is what holds the roof up.

Pole barn vs metal building

One naming point first, because it saves reading the same comparison twice: metal building and steel building are two names for the same product, so pole barn vs steel building is this comparison and not another one.

Both can wear the same steel panels, and from outside there is often nothing to tell them apart. Inside, the load path is different.

A pole barn carries the roof on posts embedded in the ground. The walls hold nothing up — they are an envelope hung on girts, which is why a post-frame building can have a wide door almost anywhere without anything being re-engineered.

A metal building carries the roof on a steel frame, normally bolted down to a slab. The frame arrives engineered as a unit, and the slab is not optional the way it is under a pole barn: it is part of the structure rather than a floor poured into a finished shell.

That gives each one a natural job. Post-frame is the cheaper route to enclosed space on open ground, because it skips the continuous foundation. Steel framing earns its keep at long clear spans and in commercial work where the engineering is expected anyway.

Where it stops being obvious: very wide buildings. A timber truss gets deep and heavy as the span grows, and at some point steel is simply the sensible material — which is the same point where post-frame stops being clearly cheaper.

Post frame vs pole barn

There is no difference. Post-frame is the industry and code term; pole barn is what people say. Both describe a building whose roof is carried by posts.

The name is worth knowing for one practical reason: permit offices, engineers and truss suppliers use post-frame, and a search for it returns technical documents while a search for pole barn returns sellers.

Pole barn vs stick built

Stick built is ordinary house framing: studs every 16 inches standing on a continuous poured foundation, sheathed and finished.

Against it, post-frame removes two of the most expensive parts of a small building. The foundation goes from a continuous footing under every wall to individual holes at 8 to 12 feet. The wall framing goes from studs at 16 inches to posts at those same wide intervals, with girts between them.

Fewer holes to dig, less concrete to pour, fewer pieces to stand up. That is the whole saving, and it is structural rather than a matter of finish quality.

What stick built still wins: interior walls that carry load, conventional insulation detailing, and a building that a residential appraiser has seen a thousand times before. For living space, that last point is worth more than it sounds.

Pole barn vs shed

A shed is a small outbuilding, usually prefabricated, often delivered whole and sometimes sitting on skids rather than anything permanent. A pole barn is built where it stands.

The line between them is not a dimension so much as a threshold. Below a certain size, a delivered shed is cheaper and faster, and no one is going to engineer it. Above it — when the building needs a permit, a real door for a vehicle, or a span nothing prefabricated covers — post-frame becomes the cheaper way to get there, and a shed scaled up stops being a shed.

Pole barn vs garage

A garage is a use, not a construction method, which is why the comparison is really pole barn garage against stick-built garage. Both hold the same cars.

The choice follows the same logic as above: attached to a house, matching its construction usually wins on appearance and appraisal; free-standing on open ground, post-frame is normally cheaper for the same floor area, especially once the door gets wide enough for something larger than a car.

Pole barn vs metal carport

A carport is a roof and nothing else. The frame is much the same idea — uprights carrying a roof — and the price gap is entirely in what a pole barn adds: walls, doors, a slab if you want one, and the option to insulate.

Which means the honest question is never carport or pole barn. It is whether you need the building closed. If the answer is no, a roof is dramatically cheaper. If the answer is yes, the roof was never the expensive part.

Choosing between them

Work backwards from three things, in this order.

Does it need a foundation for another reason? If a slab is going in anyway — a workshop floor, a commercial use — one of post-frame's advantages disappears and steel framing becomes more competitive.

How wide is the clear span? Up to ordinary widths, timber trusses on posts are the cheap answer. Past that, steel.

Is anyone going to live in it? Then the comparison is not with metal buildings at all but with stick built, and the questions are insulation detailing, interior load-bearing walls and how a lender sees the result.

FAQ

Questions & Answers

Answered from the same sources as the tables above — each figure is linked on the page it comes from.

What is the difference between a pole barn and a metal building?

What carries the load. A pole barn stands on posts embedded in the ground and needs no continuous foundation; a metal building stands on a steel frame that is normally bolted to a slab. Both can be clad in the same steel panels, which is why they look alike from outside and are priced differently.

Is post frame vs pole barn a real difference?

No — they are the same thing. Post-frame is the industry term and the one used in building codes; pole barn is what everyone actually says. A seller using both words on one page is describing one building, not two.

Pole barn vs stick built — which is cheaper?

Post-frame, in nearly every case, and the reason is structural rather than cosmetic. Stick built needs a continuous foundation and studs every 16 inches; post-frame puts posts every 8 to 12 feet and skips the foundation. Fewer holes, less concrete, fewer pieces.

What is the difference between a shed and a pole barn?

Size and how it is built. A shed is a small outbuilding, often prefabricated and delivered whole, sometimes sitting on skids. A pole barn is built in place on posts and starts where sheds stop — and once a shed gets large enough to need a permit, post-frame usually becomes the cheaper way to get there.

Pole barn vs metal carport — what is the difference?

A carport is a roof with no walls, and a pole barn is a building with them. The frame is much the same in both cases; everything that separates them in price is the envelope — walls, doors, insulation and slab.
